Privacy policy

Effective 6 October 2026.

Capsule is a Windows app that runs only on your own PC. It has no server: nothing it reads is sent to its developer or to anyone else.

Google Calendar

When you connect Google Calendar, Capsule asks Google for read-only access to your events and your list of calendars. It uses them only to show, on your own screen, when your next event starts and what is on today and tomorrow.

  • Event titles and calendar names are shown on screen only. They are kept in memory while Capsule runs, and are never written to disk, logged or sent anywhere.
  • To stay signed in, Capsule keeps the sign-in Google gives it (a refresh token) on your PC, encrypted for your Windows account. Short-lived access tokens are kept in memory only.
  • Capsule talks only to Google's own servers (oauth2.googleapis.com, www.googleapis.com and calendar.google.com) for this.
  • Capsule doesn't sell, share or transfer your Google data, use it for advertising, or let anyone read it.

Capsule's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other features

Capsule also reads your Claude Code and Codex usage from Anthropic and OpenAI, and sends ideas you type to your own Notion database, each with your own sign-in for that service. That data, too, goes only between your PC and that service.

Removing access

Choose Sign out in Capsule's settings: it deletes the saved sign-in and asks Google to revoke it. You can also remove Capsule at any time from your Google Account, under Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services. Deleting %USERPROFILE%\.capsule removes everything Capsule keeps on your PC.
